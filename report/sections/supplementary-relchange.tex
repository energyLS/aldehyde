\tocbigheading{Supplementary Discussion 3: High export sensitivity}
\label{sec:highexportsens}


In our main study, we investigate hydrogen export scenarios up to 120 TWh/a. Here, we perform a sensitivity analysis where we show the effects of hydrogen export scenarios up to 200 TWh/a.
Supplementary Fig. \ref{fig:balances-ac-200} displays the electricity supply and demand at fixed export levels and increasing domestic \co mitigation (\ref{fig:balances-ac-0exp}) and vice versa (\ref{fig:balances-ac-co2l20-200}).
The relative cost of domestic electricity and hydrogen export in high export scenarios (see Supplementary Fig. \ref{fig:expenses_default_200}) supports the findings of the main study, as domestic electricity consumers benefit from high hydrogen export especially at low domestic \co mitigation scenarios. 
When taking high export scenarios (up to 200 TWh/a) into account, the effect of temporal hydrogen regulation on electricity and hydrogen costs is stronger compared to the main study, as displayed in Supplementary Fig. \ref{fig:expenses-relative-200}.
